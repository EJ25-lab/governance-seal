You can’t trend what won’t line up.
Three formulas, three calendars, three reporting systems — plus one-time money (like ESSER) hiding inside the federal chain. Because they never meet on one cadence, a board can’t trend them together: local effort can quietly drop as state or federal rises, and no single report would show it. That is the shell game. The instrument that trends all three on one cadence — the Money Panel — is what makes it visible; the per-pupil separation below is the first guardrail.

The shell game, in Roanoke’s own record
This isn’t hypothetical. Roanoke’s FY2026-27 board minutes show every piece of it on the record.
One-time money masked the base
~$97.5M in pandemic relief (2020–24) bridged a standing ~$10M structural deficit. When it ended, the gap surfaced — a $14–16.5M deficit (Jan–May 2026).
Local choice sits above the state floor
The cuts fell on “services above SOQ” — activity buses, a gifted pull-out, point-to-point transport — the local-choice layer on top of the Required Local Effort minimum.
State money comes with conditions
An average 2% raise was “required to qualify for state compensation funding” — a state incentive the local budget must reach up to unlock.
No cushion left to absorb it
A first-ever 0.5% contingency ($1.3M) was set because the division “no longer operates with an independent fund balance” — the shock absorber is gone.

The federal chain isn’t 0.7%. It’s hiding.
That $3.1M is only Impact Aid, ROTC, and Medicaid. Title I, IDEA, Title II, Title III, and Perkins — the $25.5M this analysis already locked — are nowhere in the operating budget. They live in separate grant funds, approved in a different board action, not in the book the public reviews at the hearing. So the public budget reads about 8× too low on federal. The operating book and the grant funds are two different sets of books — which is why the money “comes out in a session the public can’t see.” The Money Panel is the one view that puts both books on the same page.

The state chain — SOQ, lottery & categoricals

Virginia state money arrives as SOQ per-ADM funding (Basic Aid + the state sales-tax share) plus lottery-funded and categorical/incentive accounts. EIRI — the Early Intervention Reading Initiative — is one categorical among many, and like several it requires a local match. The local chain — required effort + local choice

Local money is two parts: the Required Local Effort (RLE) — the minimum the state formula obligates the locality to contribute — and everything the city appropriates above it. It is the most flexible chain, and the one supplement-not-supplant exists to protect: federal money may not quietly replace it.
Newport News — the split, to the dollar
The City appropriates $133,659,402.00. The state formula only requires $66,311,560 (Required Local Effort, at Composite Index 0.2826). So $67,347,842 is local choice above the floor — Newport News invests roughly double what the state obligates. FY2027 budget, pp. 37 & 87.
Roanoke — topline in hand
FY2026-27 general fund ~$269.9M; the City’s school appropriation ~$106.9M (2025-26). This year’s cuts fell on services above the SOQ minimum — the local-choice layer. The exact RLE split is a line in its adopted budget.

The base, and the money on top

Every federal dollar in this analysis is a supplement — it lays on top of the state and local money that funds every student, and it is restricted to the group it names. Per-pupil expenditure is what separates the two, and that separation is the guardrail: federal money must add to the base, never replace it.

For every ~$15,000–$17,000 of state+local a student already receives, the targeted federal grants add roughly $1,000 per pupil — about 6%. That is the whole doctrine in one picture: a thin, restricted layer on top of the base.
What supplanting looks like
Using Title I to pay for something state/local money already funded — so the base quietly drops as federal money arrives.
The guardrails
Supplement-not-supplant tests, maintenance-of-effort, comparability, and time-and-effort records — the base must stay whole and the money must reach the named group.

It’s not the kids.
The child in three categories is not the finding — the finding is whether the funded, published system reached them. The poverty count, the EL count, and the disability count all point to the same student, and to the same question: did we do what we published? Every one of these plans is public. A board can read them, and the Status Board is what lets it see the whole child — not three disconnected rows.

Portfolio composition — deep read

Two findings a board should see. First, the money is concentrated: Title I and IDEA are ~89% of the federal portfolio, and the EL money (Title III) is the smallest stream of all. Second, every stream scales with district size — Newport News runs ~1.7–1.8× Roanoke — except Title III, which is 1.0×, because it follows English-learner count, and the two boards enroll almost the same number.

✦ Title III is the tell: it does not scale with size because it scales with need (EL count). Every formula stream answers a different population — poverty, disability, EL, CTE — so “how much federal money” is the wrong question. Which need, how much per child, and is it working is the right one.
